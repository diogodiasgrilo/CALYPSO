## How to use this doc

Each candidate gets 5 scores, **0–10, higher is always better/more attractive** (no dimension is inverted, so you never have to remember "low is good" for any column). There's no blessed formula for combining them into one number — different scores matter more or less depending on what you're optimizing for at the time (fast to ship vs. genuinely diversifying vs. don't want a 6th thing to babysit). The summary table includes a simple unweighted average purely for rough sorting; treat it as a tiebreaker, not a verdict.

### Scoring rubric

| # | Dimension | 10 means... | 0 means... |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Implementation Ease** | Nearly a config/parameter change on existing code paths | Needs a new execution model or data source built from scratch |
| 2 | **Differentiation** | Fundamentally different structure/edge from every strategy HYDRA already runs (A/B/C 0DTE credit ICs, D/E multi-day net-debit calendars) | Essentially a re-skin of an existing variant — not worth running in parallel |
| 3 | **Uncorrelatedness** | Genuinely uncorrelated P&L from variant B (the live seat) — would actually diversify fleet-level risk | Same underlying risk exposure as B in different clothing (e.g. still short 0DTE gamma, hurts on B's worst days too) |
| 4 | **Claim Credibility** | Independently verified/backtested by someone other than the strategy's own creator | Single creator's unaudited word only, nothing else found anywhere |
| 5 | **Operational Overhead** | Zero new dependencies — fits the current monitoring loop, entitlements, and broker capabilities as-is | Needs a whole new subsystem, market-data entitlement, or underlying |

Dimensions 3 and 4 were added on top of what was originally asked for (ease + "not worth doing if too similar," which became Differentiation) — flagged for your review, drop or reweight either if they don't matter to you:
- **Uncorrelatedness** exists because "different legs" doesn't mean "different risk." Something can look mechanically novel and still just be short-vol-in-a-trenchcoat — this dimension catches that.
- **Claim Credibility** exists because every strategy sourced from the Theta Profits ecosystem is a single creator's self-reported, unaudited numbers. Worth knowing which claims have *any* outside scrutiny before betting real capital on the premise.

### 21 DTE Put Broken Wing Butterfly

**Source:** Carl Allen (Data Driven Options) — [thetaprofits.com interview](https://www.thetaprofits.com/broken-wing-butterfly-a-high-probability-options-strategy/) (John Einar Sandvand, published 2025-03-30) and Allen's own detailed methodology page, [datadrivenoptions.com](https://datadrivenoptions.com/strategies-for-option-trading/favorite-strategies/broken-wing-put-butterfly/) (posted 2021, actively maintained through March 2026, 64 comments — fetched via raw `curl`+manual extraction, since WebFetch's summary missed the comment thread entirely, which turned out to hold the richest evidence found this pass) plus the original ["500% in a year"](https://datadrivenoptions.com/500-return-from-broken-wing-butterfly-options/) source post (2021-12-07).

**Trader background:** Self-described "experienced retail trader from Kansas City," former Procter & Gamble employee (a real corporate career, not a trading credential — no CTA/RIA/fund registration found). **A name-collision risk was caught and avoided this pass**: a LinkedIn "Carl Allen" in St Paul, MN surfaces high in search results but is a different person entirely (CEO of a school-transportation startup, ex-Peace Corps) — do not conflate the two. One genuine point in his favor versus most peers in this doc: he doesn't monetize this specific strategy (no alert service, no paid course — told a commenter asking to pay for alerts "there is no alert service... the whole point of this site is to help people make trading decisions for themselves"). **Currency flag:** as of his most recent 2026 comments, Allen lists his *current* top-3 traded strategies as rolling put credit spreads, rolling covered calls, and rolling double diagonals — the 21 DTE BWB isn't among them, and he stated in 2024 "I haven't traded this strategy consistently this year."

**Structure:** All-put asymmetric butterfly on SPX — buy 1 put ~32Δ (highest strike), sell 2 puts ~28Δ (middle strike), buy 1 put ~21Δ (lowest strike), wide wing built 2x the narrow wing. Opened at **21 DTE** (not 0DTE — a multi-day, single-expiry hold, typically closed ~7 DTE, a ~14-day average hold). Entry targets 12–15% of the narrow spread's width in premium (Allen's own site says "10% or more," and a 2024 comment cites a real "20×40 wings" example — parameter drift across his own re-tellings over time, a mild curve-fit flag). Profit target: a standing GTC order to close at 2% of narrow-spread width, placed immediately on entry. Stop: precisely, the trigger is 2× credit collected, but the *total debit* at that trigger is 3× credit (a detail even his own commenters routinely mis-parse). His actually-preferred alternative to stopping out is "rolling the losing side" — splitting the butterfly into its constituent spreads, selling the now-ITM debit spread, and rolling the credit spread out (and often down) in time, which roughly doubles the buying-power requirement and can take weeks to resolve.

**Claimed performance:** "80% win rate" (drops to ~70% with his recommended stop), "often 10% return in two weeks." The flagship "500% in a year" claim, traced to its exact origin: a single $9,000→$54,000 account over one year starting June 2020 — the immediate post-COVID melt-up regime, carrying ~50% cash "to fight downturns," with a more honest 119%-ex-compounding figure buried in the same letter. This claim is now 5 years old, tied to one unusually favorable regime, and has never been repeated with fresh data.

**Independent verification — substantially richer than the prior pass found, three separate analyses, all surfaced only by reading the raw comment threads on Allen's own two sites:** (1) a Monte Carlo backtest (1M iterations, no stop) found a **negative** average EV of −$3.75/trade; with Allen's recommended 2× stop (500K iterations): win rate 72.3% (matches his claim), EV +$0.92/trade "thanks to active management," Sharpe 1.85, ~3.5%/cycle real return — not the marketed 10%/2wk. The same commenter's conclusion, quoted: *"it's a strategy that is on the edge with a very low EV."* (2) A second commenter ran a rough back-of-envelope EV calc using Allen's own quoted numbers and got a near-zero/negative result — Allen's reply is the single most damaging line found in this whole research project, on a site literally named "Data Driven Options": *"I also spoke without having the data, which is clearly not data driven."* (3) A third-party 30-DTE symmetric put-butterfly backtest (unrelated site, different structure) corroborates the general shape's regime-dependence (weaker in genuine bear markets), echoing Allen's own caveat. Net: the ~70-80% win-rate claim roughly survives scrutiny; the marketed *return* does not.

**Codebase grounding:** Broker-level primitives are solid reuse — `qualify_option_strikes()`/`get_option_greeks()` are DTE-agnostic and delta-based (no Polygon dependency), `place_order()` takes arbitrary per-leg quantity so the 1-2-1 ratio is a parameter. **Correction to the original pass:** `target_dte` is *not* hardcoded to 0 — `base_strategy.py:6863` already reads it from config and `_get_todays_expiry()` already shifts the expiry forward when `target_dte > 0` (shipped 2026-06-08). But a more concrete blocker was found in its place: **`STATE-004`'s overnight-position halt is a live, currently-unresolved go-live gate** — it's the literal, documented reason Strategy D itself remains `dry_run_locked` today (three MUST-FIXes: scope the halt/orphan-sweep to the strategy's own conids, budget per-variant buying power — none shipped). A 21 DTE BWB can't inherit D/E's two-expiry-specific workaround and would face this identical open problem from scratch. `docs/NEW_STRATEGY_PLAYBOOK.md`'s Step-0 table (read in full — it actually has 3 rows, not 2) confirms this is squarely a **Heavy build**. One new operational risk: `_get_todays_expiry` is weekday-aware only, not exchange-holiday-aware — irrelevant to 0DTE, real for anything spanning a holiday.

**Scores:** Ease 5 · Differentiation 8 · Uncorrelatedness 5 · Credibility 3 · Overhead 4 — **Verdict: WORTH_CONSIDERING** (average unchanged at 5.0 — two score corrections roughly offset: `target_dte` turned out already fixed, but `STATE-004` turned out to be a sharper, currently-open blocker; see Revision History)

### METF (Multiple Entry Trend Following)

**Source:** Dan Yaklin — [thetaprofits.com](https://www.thetaprofits.com/how-to-trade-the-metf-0dte-options-strategy/) (interview by John Einar Sandvand, byline dated September 29, 2024 — the oldest source article of any strategy in this doc by a wide margin, most of which are dated 2026; also trades MEIC alongside this, for a smoother combined curve). Raw HTML pulled directly (bypassing WebFetch summarization) to check for omitted detail and a comment thread, per the same method used on Skyline.

**Trader background — thinner than most peers in this doc:** The article's only biographical claim is "a veteran retail trader from Dallas, Texas, USA" who "uses Trade Automation Toolbox to enter his trades" and "runs several accounts." A dedicated search (LinkedIn, SEC IAPD, NFA/CTA registry, any other publication or business site under his own name) found **nothing verifiable** — the LinkedIn hits for "Dan Yaklin" resolve to unrelated people (a U.S. Navy Reserve recruiter, a UPS preloader), there is no SEC/NFA registration, and — unlike Skyline's Sorabh Khurana, who at least runs his own business site (theoptionsengineer.com) with a named corporate LinkedIn history — Yaklin has **no independent site, no other publication, and no verifiable professional identity anywhere** beyond this one Theta Profits article. The article does disclose one candid risk detail unprompted: asked "what's the worst that can happen," Yaklin describes a same-day black-swan scenario losing the full spread width on all six open positions, and says "I do not risk any more than 50% of my account" as a result — a real point of transparency, but it doesn't substitute for a verifiable track record.

**Structure (verbatim from the raw article):** Six fixed SPX 0DTE entries at **"12:30, 1:00, 1:30, 2:00, 2:30, and 2:45 Eastern Standard Time."** Direction: **"If the 20 EMA is above the 40 EMA, he will sell a put credit spread. And opposite: If the 20 EMA is below the 40 EMA, he will sell a call credit spread"** — one-minute-interval EMA, confirmed in the comments ("It is using 1-minute periods"). Width **"25 – 35"** points, credit target **"$1.25 – $2.5."** Stop mechanics, precisely: **"A stop loss is set at 1X the premium received... If the credit spread is sold for $2, the stop loss will be set at $4... The loss will then be equal to the premium received, in this case 2 dollars. ... This gives a risk-reward ratio of 1:1."** So the stop *trigger price* is 2× the entry credit, but the realized *loss* at that trigger is exactly 1× the entry credit — tighter, not looser, than the prior pass's "stop ≈ 2x credit received" implied on its own if read as the loss amount. Late in the day, once the long legs are worthless, the stop migrates to the short leg only. Dan is "100% mechanical" — same six clock times every day, no discretionary override, specifically because he runs several accounts and hasn't seen discretionary traders in the same ecosystem beat his fixed-time results side by side.

**Claimed performance:** Creator's claim, one year, Jun 2023–Jun 2024: **"Compound Annual Rate of Return (CAR) of 50%,"** max drawdown **"8.5%,"** Calmar **"5.98%"** vs. **"2.15%"** for buy-and-hold SPX over the same window (self-rated risk 5/10). No win rate, no trade count, no equity-curve data beyond a chart shown only in the (untranscribed) video.

**Independent verification — more exists than the quick pass found, but none of it verifies Yaklin's own numbers, and what's there argues for a thinner, more lopsided edge than the marketing implies:**
- **The Theta Profits comment thread itself (10 comments, read in full via raw HTML) has zero skeptical scrutiny** — a sharp contrast with Skyline's thread on the same site. Every comment is a clarifying question (EMA timeframe, whether METF+MEIC double up on one side, stop-loss mechanics for MEIC) with Sandvand or a reader answering; nobody attempted a replication or disputed the numbers.
- **Cashflow Engine** ([cashflowengine.io](https://cashflowengine.io/learn/metf-multiple-entries-trend-following/), author Thomas Mehlitz, "Founder & Trading System Engineer") is a genuinely separate commercial site (own paid backtest/automation product, "generally credit[s]" the strategy to Yaklin without any promotional tie to him) that ran an independent large-scale backtest: **19,512,000 simulated SPX 0DTE credit-spread trades, second-level (1-second) options data, Jul 2024–Jun 2026, across 19,620 parameter combinations per side.** It broadly *supports* the core mechanic — trades placed on the EMA-favored side beat the "wrong side" bucket — but the magnitude is telling: **call credit spreads sold correctly (downtrend state) averaged +$24.61/trade at 63.7% win rate, while put credit spreads sold correctly (uptrend state) averaged only +$7.44/trade at 62.2%** — a **3.3× asymmetry never mentioned anywhere in the Theta Profits article.** Worse for the "wrong side" case: puts sold against the trend averaged **−$6.67/trade (net losing)**, while calls sold against the trend still averaged +$7.37 (merely worse, not negative) — CFE's own explanation is that SPX declines are fast/violent (rich premium, crushes wrong-side puts) while rallies are slow/grinding (erodes, doesn't destroy, wrong-side calls). CFE's own disclaimer explicitly flags selection bias in any single "best" parameter cell. This is real outside quantitative work, but it corroborates a much thinner, asymmetric, put-side-weak version of the edge than "50% CAR, 8.5% DD" suggests, using a materially different parameter band ($1.25–$4.00 credit, 10–200pt width — not Yaklin's exact $1.25–$2.50 / 25–35pt) — so treat it as evidence for the *strategy family*, not a check on Yaklin's specific track record.
- **options1k.com** is an anonymous individual retail trader's own blog running this exact strategy (his own tuned variant: $0.85–$1.25 credit, 40–70pt width, 100% stop, $30k account) since at least Jan 2025. This is real, unaffiliated, non-cherry-picked live evidence: the site's public monthly P&L table shows real **losing months** (Feb −$1,697.91, May −$1,516.14, Aug −$1,383.13, Oct −$78.31, Nov −$475.62, Dec −$1,956.65 in 2025 alone, mixed across his full 0DTE book, of which METF is one of several strategies). His Aug 2025 post-mortem explicitly names "METF/EMA Strategy" as contributing a "medium loss" that month (dollar figure not isolated). A week-level post from Oct 2024 captures the real texture better than any promotional claim: **"0DTE salvaged my week. It killed me last week. I have very volatile feelings about it so far... It has had pretty consistent returns over the past 5 years, which I like."** This is a real, independent, warts-and-included data point that flatly does not exist for most Theta Profits strategies — but it's one anonymous small account, not an audit, and runs different parameters than Yaklin's.
- No Reddit, no forum teardown, no Elite-Trader-style critical thread (the kind found for Sandvand's own 0DTE Breakeven IC) was found anywhere for METF specifically.

**Codebase grounding — extends the prior pass with the single most important finding of this pass: HYDRA already ran this exact mechanic once, and killed it.** `bots/hydra/strategy.py`'s `_get_trend_signal()` (`ema_short_period`/`ema_long_period` default `20`/`40`, `bots/hydra/strategy.py:340-341`) does compute the crossover live from SPX 1-min bars on every entry, and one-sided placement (`put_only`/`call_only`, MKT-032/039/040) is live infra B/C use daily — the prior pass's "connect a wire that already exists" framing is directionally right. But `bots/hydra/__init__.py`'s own version history shows that wire *used to be connected*: HYDRA's very first incarnation (v1.0.0, 2026-02-04, then named "MEIC-TF") forced entry direction from exactly this 20/40 EMA crossover — i.e., ran METF's core mechanic natively — for about three and a half weeks, until **v1.4.0 (2026-02-27) explicitly reverted it: "disable all one-sided entries (EMA signal informational only, always full IC or skip)."** `docs/HYDRA_TRADING_JOURNAL.md`'s "Weakness 1" write-up names the reason: **"EMA Trend Filter Whipsaws on V-Shaped Days"** — "20/40 EMA on 1-min bars has ~20-40 min lag," V-shape reversals "occur on ~15-20% of trading days," and when the trend filter is wrong, "entire premium is lost" (Weakness 3: "4 call-only entries: 3 stopped (75%). 2 put-only trend entries: 2 stopped (100%)"). Two dated, quantified incidents are on record: **Feb 17, 2026** — "BEARISH at the low (10:05), BULLISH at the high (11:35), both wrong"; and **Feb 26, 2026** — a BEARISH signal at 10:35 correctly read a sharp intraday downtrend, but the market reversed within 40 minutes and the resulting call-only entry got stopped, with the journal's own reconstruction estimating **"the BEARISH signal cost an estimated ~$1,000 vs the full IC alternative"** that single day. Two of the deferred mitigations from that post-mortem (Rec 9.4 "Trend Persistence Requirement," Rec 9.5 "Intraday Range Awareness") were never built — they're still listed "Deferred" six months later. This means a from-scratch METF build isn't "flip a switch": it's knowingly re-enabling a mechanic this codebase tried and walked back for a documented, dollar-quantified reason, and doing it safely would mean building the anti-whipsaw guards that were scoped in Feb 2026 and never shipped — a real (if modest, ~15-50 lines per the journal's own complexity estimates) addition the prior pass's "ease 8" didn't account for.

Two things *do* remain genuinely easy, confirmed by reading the current files fresh: (1) **leg shape needs nothing new** — METF's 2-leg vertical is a strict subset of the existing 4-name `Leg` model (`bots/hydra/leg.py` `LEG_NAMES = ("short_call", "long_call", "short_put", "long_put")`), identical in shape to the one-sided entries B/C already place daily; no new Entry/LegSet class is needed, unlike every multi-leg candidate elsewhere in this doc. (2) **Taxonomy/dashboard need nothing new either** — `shared/strategy_taxonomy.py` already tags A/B/C `structure_family="iron_condor", pnl_shape="credit"`, and METF's always-one-sided `IronCondorEntry` is the same shape the dashboard's credit-IC renderer already has to handle for B/C's daily `put_only`/`call_only` entries; `docs/NEW_STRATEGY_PLAYBOOK.md`'s own Step-0 table classifies "Another 0DTE credit structure" as **Light** (reuse `IronCondorEntry`, base settlement, `backtesting.db`; skip the sidecar/isolated-DB/new-taxonomy steps D/E needed) — an unambiguous fit, unlike most other candidates in this doc that are edge cases the table doesn't cleanly cover. Genuinely new, confirmed by reading `_calculate_stop_levels_hydra` (`bots/hydra/strategy.py:8518`) directly: every existing stop path is credit+buffer (full IC: call `$0.75`/put `$1.75` buffer; call-only even prices in a theoretical `$2.60` put) — none of them match METF's flat "trigger at 2×credit, lose exactly 1×credit" rule, so that stop formula is still new code, not a config flip. `MKT-047`'s EOD-flatten already operates per-side (not per-entry), so it needs no changes for a strategy that's always one-sided.

**Scores:** Ease 7 · Differentiation 3 · Uncorrelatedness 3 · Credibility 3 · Overhead 7 — **Verdict: WEAK_CANDIDATE** (average 4.6, down slightly from the first pass's 4.8 — see revision history for what moved and why)

### Boomer Dan — 0DTE Levitation / Burrito Butterfly

**Source:** Dan Westbrook ("Boomer Dan") — [Levitation article](https://www.thetaprofits.com/0dte-levitation-trades-how-boomer-dan-creates-risk-free-spx-profits-by-the-end-of-the-day/) (Sandvand, 2025-11-09) and [Burrito Butterfly article](https://www.thetaprofits.com/burrito-butterfly-boomer-dans-unique-twist-on-the-butterfly-spread/) (2026-06-14). **Correction to the original pass's own flagged uncertainty:** `KuQdL4-1IcM` (the video originally researched) is definitively the **Burrito Butterfly** companion video ("This butterfly strategy aims to remove risk as fast as possible," upload date matches the article exactly), not Levitation as assumed. Levitation's real companion is a separate video, [`DHViE1YZ710`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DHViE1YZ710) ("The 0DTE strategy with zero risk?"). Full auto-caption transcripts of **both** videos were obtained this pass via `yt-dlp` (already installed in the project venv) — the original pass said a transcript "was never obtainable"; it was, via a tool that just hadn't been tried.

**Trader background:** Self-described Hollywood film/video editor through the 1990s–2000s (Warner Bros, New Line, Sony trailers), self-taught options trader since ~2006 after a stray mention in a Robert Kiyosaki book. No LinkedIn or other verifiable professional identity found in a dedicated search. Monetizes via a free eBook, free Discord, a free "Trading Skool" (2.6K members), and — a new finding — an **affiliate arrangement promoting a different trader's ($1,495) course**, featuring his own personal testimonial as the page's social proof.

**Structure — 0DTE Levitation:** ATM SPX put (or call) credit spread (worked example: 5pt wide, short 6695/long 6690, $2.55 collected). Hedge (first 1-2hrs): either a ~$1 same-day OTM option, or his *stated preference* — one MES futures contract, chosen because it carries no vega/theta decay. Once profitable, "flips" into a butterfly by adding an opposing debit spread whose short strike matches the original short exactly (worked example: $103 profit → adds a debit spread for $1.30 → **$125 guaranteed minimum profit** either direction). **A detail the original article-only pass missed entirely:** the video makes clear this is only his simplest variant — he explicitly says the same flip works starting from a *full iron condor* too, much closer to what A/B/C already run than the single-sided framing suggested. Once levitated, he **stacks additional conversions all day** ("a wall of profit tents... six or seven butterflies... all lined up") and separately buys back cheap decayed outer wings.

**Structure — Burrito Butterfly:** 15pt-wide ATM butterfly + 5pt directional debit spread. Per Dan's own words, tested at "zero DTE, one DTE, seven DTEs, 14 DTEs... best results 14 days or under." Adjustment techniques if wrong: a stop "uncle point," adding the opposite debit spread anyway, buying a cheap flattening option, the "choo-choo train" (stacking more butterflies), or the "clawback" (trading in **XSP**, a separate 1/10-size SPX-linked product, to fine-tune the underwater zones).

This is not a hypothetical risk; it's HYDRA's own dated, dollar-quantified result for this precise leg shape, arguably more directly on point here than for the doc's SPX Put Credit Spread entry (that one is multi-day/5-7 DTE with an ATM short strike; this one is 0DTE at a 10-25Δ short strike — closer in regime to the actual MKT-039/040 trades the memory was measured from).
- **Two existing safety filters are philosophically opposed to this strategy's entire thesis, though both are just per-variant config knobs, not hardcoded blockers.** The Anti-Whipsaw filter (`strategy.py:3735-3760`, config `whipsaw_range_skip_mult`) explicitly **skips** entries when `intraday_range > 1.75 × expected_move` — i.e., it exists to avoid entering during exactly the "SPX at its expected-move boundary" condition that IS Ghauri's entry trigger. A dedicated variant would need its own config (`whipsaw_range_skip_mult: null`, calm-entry disabled) rather than inheriting A/B/C's defaults — mechanically simple since both are already per-variant knobs, but a reminder that these filters exist because HYDRA's own operating experience says entering into big moves is dangerous for a short-premium book, which is precisely this strategy's business model.
- **The entry TRIGGER is genuinely new control flow, though modest in scope.** Every current HYDRA entry fires off a fixed time-of-day list (`entry_times`, `_parse_entry_times`); nothing in the codebase continuously watches for "has price touched a level today" as a fire condition. This needs a new per-tick check (reusing the existing monitoring-loop cadence in `main.py:run_bot()`) plus new state ("has this boundary already fired today, which side") — a real but scoped addition, not a new execution model. Delta-based strike selection has a directly reusable precedent in `brandon/strategy.py`'s `delta_target_strike_selection` (`target_delta_pct`, ~lines 318-361) anchored off live greeks (`shared/ib_client.py:2326`, `get_option_greeks`) — Ghauri's 10-25Δ target maps onto the same mechanism Brandon already uses for its own 8Δ target.
- **`STATE-004` does not apply here** — unlike the SPX Put Credit Spread entry above (5-7 DTE, multi-day) or a Strategy-D-style calendar, Ghauri's structure is explicitly 0DTE and closes same-session (avg 20-30 min hold, no new entries after 1 PM ET, and the position expires or is closed same-day regardless) — so it never touches the overnight-position halt that keeps Strategy D at NO-GO. A genuine architectural advantage over this doc's other one-sided-credit-spread entry.

Rationale: **Ease** is the highest of any candidate in this doc with non-trivial differentiation, precisely because the leg shape needs zero new data model (2 of the existing 4 `Leg` slots), the EM formula is already computed every tick, and delta-targeting has a working precedent in Brandon — the only real new code is an event-driven trigger layered onto the existing monitoring loop, not a new execution model. **Differentiation** is middling, not high: the entry trigger (event-driven fade vs. scheduled) and short average hold (20-30 min vs. HYDRA's hold-to-close-or-stop) are real mechanical differences from A/B/C, but the resulting position is structurally identical to a leg shape HYDRA already places today (MKT-039/040 one-sided fallback) — this isn't a new structure, it's a new *reason* to deliberately and permanently choose an existing one. **Uncorrelatedness is the pivotal, carefully-weighed score, and it cuts against the diversification premise:** on ordinary reversion/whipsaw days, fading a move that snaps back plausibly would diversify away from B's schedule-driven book. But on the rare, high-impact trend-continuation days that matter most for portfolio tail risk — SPX blows through the expected move and keeps going, never reverting — this strategy loses for the *same underlying reason* B's short-gamma 0DTE book loses on those days (a sustained one-directional 0DTE move against a short-premium position), just entered later and more aggressively into the move rather than earlier and schedule-blind. HYDRA's own `onesided_entry_negative_expectancy` finding shows this exact leg shape's losses are structural and trend-day-concentrated, not random noise — the same failure mode this strategy would be betting against, on purpose, on every single trade. **Credibility** is low: a real independent backtest exists and shows modest positive EV, but flatly contradicts the specific win-rate claim, and the trader's own managed fund is independently documented (Bogleheads, corroborated by two separate YouTube commenters) with marketed returns that don't match real account statements — serious enough to weigh against trusting any of Ghauri's self-reported numbers. **Overhead** is the highest score in this entry: no new instrument, no new data entitlement (SPX/VIX are already real-time-subscribed), no new external dependency — the cost is entirely in new (but modest) code, not new operational surface.
