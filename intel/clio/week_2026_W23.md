## Part 3: Strategy Recommendations

### Recommendation 1: Lower the Regime 0 call credit floor from $1.00 to $0.75 (or implement SPX-level-adjusted scaling)

**Data:** The $1.00 Regime 0 call minimum (`vix_regime.min_call_credit[0]`) was calibrated when SPX was approximately 200-300 points lower (per Apollo Jun 2 briefing and W23 CLIO report). At SPX 7,500+, MKT-024's 2.5× OTM multiplier places short calls ~40-44pt OTM where VIX 15-16 generates sub-$1.00 premium on 75pt-wide spreads (from W23 Strategy Memory). This produced: 0 entries on 05-29 (all 5 attempts gated), 0 entries on 06-01 (though MKT-010 was the proximate cause, the underlying premium drought is the same dynamic), 1 of 3 entries on 06-03 (E#1 gated, E#2 cleared at exactly $1.00 — the floor was binding), and thin credits on both placed entries on 06-04 ($112.50 and $107.50).

**Proposed change:** `vix_regime.min_call_credit[0]` from `1.00` to `0.75`, with effective floor from `0.90` to `0.65`. The $0.75 target aligns with what the system can actually generate at 2.5× OTM at current SPX/VIX levels while preserving meaningful premium over commission. Alternatively, implement a dynamic floor: `min_call_credit = min(1.00, 0.012 × VIX)` (at VIX 16: 0.192 → too low; perhaps `0.06 × VIX` → at VIX 16: $0.96, at VIX 15: $0.90) — but a simple flat reduction to $0.75 is the more immediately testable change.

**Expected impact:** On 06-03, Entry #1 at 10:45 was skipped on MKT-011 call credit gate. If the floor were $0.75, it likely would have cleared given that the same entry at 10:47 cleared at $1.45 the day prior at slightly different SPX. More importantly, the systematic skip cascade on 05-29 and near-cascade on 06-01 may have been partially avoided. Quantified opportunity cost: skipped entries over the trailing 5 trading days (05-29 through 06-01) represent at least 5 foregone entry attempts that would have needed only $0.75 to clear — hard to quantify exactly, but the premium desert weeks have net -$50 to $0 outcomes vs. the potential for +$400-600 on clean sweep days.

**Confidence: MEDIUM.** The structural argument is strong, but reducing the floor increases tail risk on the call side (accepting thinner premium for the same spread width means the risk/reward ratio worsens). MKT-020/022 tightening already compensates by moving strikes inward until credit is met — at a lower floor, this tightening would be less extreme, meaning strikes stay further OTM but at lower premium. The June 4 call stop on E#1 at only 60pt OTM (thin credit after tightening) illustrates that tighter MKT-020/022 iteration can produce dangerously tight placements even at the current $1.00 floor. A parallel shadow test at $0.75 before live deployment is warranted.

**Config edit:** `vix_regime.min_call_credit[0]` from `1.00` → `0.75`; `vix_regime.call_credit_floor[0]` from `0.90` → `0.65`.

### Recommendation 2: Widen Z0 stop buffers by elevating them to their own per-regime values rather than relying on global fallbacks

**Data:** Every day of W24 operated under Z0 stop buffers ($0.75 call / $1.75 put — global fallback). The strategy context notes Z0 uses global fallback because "n=5 days" at the time of the Option B deployment (2026-04-27). As of W24, the cumulative_metrics records show at least 10+ Regime 0 days post-deployment (April–June, VIX <18 days including the extensive May–June run). The Z1 widened buffers ($1.50 call / $2.50 put) only kick in at VIX ≥18, which HYDRA almost never reaches now. The June 5 put stops on both E#1 and E#2 at -$831.10 with global put buffer $1.75 suggests the buffer was insufficient for a 164pt move — both short puts at ~7,450-7,455 were breached by 80-86pt.

**Proposed change:** Set explicit `vix_regime.call_stop_buffer[0]` = `1.00` and `vix_regime.put_stop_buffer[0]` = `2.00` (midway between Z0 global and Z1 per-regime values) rather than relying on the global fallback `null`. This acknowledges that Z0 has sufficient sample now (10+ days post-Apr 27) for independent calibration. Whether wider buffers would have prevented June 5 stops is unclear — the 164pt move would have blown through any reasonable buffer — but at a smaller drawdown event, wider Z0 buffers would reduce noise-triggered stops.

**Confidence: LOW-MEDIUM.** The June 5 event was a 164pt outlier (2× the typical Regime 0 expected move) where no stop buffer change would have materially altered the outcome. The case for widening Z0 buffers is primarily about reducing noise stops on smaller moves in Regime 0, not preventing the June 5 scenario. Given that Z0 is now the dominant regime and the global fallback was established with only n=5 days, the 4-week review trigger mentioned in `HYDRA_BUFFER_OPTIMIZATION.md` appears overdue. The sample is now sufficient for independent calibration.

**Config edit:** `vix_regime.call_stop_buffer[0]` from `null` → `1.00`; `vix_regime.put_stop_buffer[0]` from `null` → `2.00`. Trigger a formal Z0 buffer review using all Regime 0 days from Apr 27 onward.

### Recommendation 3: Flag the recurring Upday-035 / Downday-035 put-credit zero-floor failure for structural review

**Data:** The Upday-035 conditional at Entry #3 (14:00 put-only) triggered on 06-01 and 06-04, and **both times found $0.00 put credit** against the $1.15 effective put floor, producing a skip. On 06-01: SPX was +0.67% above open at 14:00 (~+51pt), yet put credit was $0.00. On 06-04: SPX was +0.75% above open at 14:00 (~+56pt), same result. The Upday-035 logic is sound (bullish drift → sell puts), but when SPX has already risen significantly, near-the-money put premium collapses to near zero. The entry fires, evaluates, and skips — consuming a slot but generating no revenue.

**Structural issue:** The put credit floor of $1.15 (effective Regime 0) may be appropriate for a fresh put-only entry but is calibrated for earlier-in-day conditions when IV is higher. At 14:00 with SPX 50+ points above open in Regime 0, put premium has decayed substantially — both IV crush from the directional move and time decay over the full morning combine. The Downday-035 equivalent (call-only at 14:00) worked correctly on June 5 because calls remain better bid when market is falling (elevated put IV doesn't help call premium directly, but the general vol spike helps).

**Proposed change:** Consider implementing a separate, lower put credit floor for the 14:00 Upday-035 conditional slot — e.g., `upday_035_min_put_credit` = $0.75 (vs. the standard $1.15), recognizing that premium at 14:00 on a rally day is structurally thinner than at 10:45-11:15. Alternatively, reduce the `put_credit_floor` at Regime 0 specifically for conditional entries.

**Expected impact:** Would have potentially converted 2 skipped entries (06-01 and 06-04 E#3) into placed entries. At $0.75 minimum credit, the P&L contribution would be modest but positive on clean expiry days. However, the $0.00 quote on 06-01 suggests even $0.75 may not have been achievable — HERMES notes the 14:00 credit as literally $0.00.

**Confidence: LOW.** A $0.00 quote cannot be cleared by any floor reduction — MKT-011 requires actual credit availability. The structural issue is not the floor but the fundamental absence of viable put premium after a rally. A lower floor would help only on days where premium is $0.25-$0.75 (viable but sub-floor). Without the actual mid-price quotes at 14:00 on the skip days, it's impossible to confirm this range. Flag for data collection: log the actual mid-price quote (even sub-floor) at Entry #3 Upday-035/Downday-035 skips.
