## Part 4: New Learnings for Strategy Memory

1. **Sep 30 (VIX 15.91, SPX open 7,692.81, session −26.74pt): The delta-target floor gate (4.0δ minimum) produces a complete grid whitout when sustained intraday SPX selling pushes the market away from the 8δ-anchored put strike, making far-OTM chains under-hydrated.** On Sep 30, all 7 slots failed because the ~7,590 put anchor returned 0.9δ–1.4δ — not because of credit gates or GEX cascade. This is the second confirmed delta-floor-induced whitout (first documented in W30), and it occurs specifically when: (a) SPX drifts lower from open ≥20pt AND (b) the 8δ delta anchor stays fixed relative to the original open rather than adjusting to the current SPX level. The existing fallback to parent `_calculate_strikes` is not triggering in this scenario, confirming the W30 diagnosis that "sparse but non-null" far-OTM greeks prevent the outage-triggered fallback from firing.

2. **Oct 01 (VIX 16.52, ES gap +90.7pt, cash open 7,672.20, session high 7,684.37): When ES gap ≥ 85pt corrects to a cash open below 7,685, the 8δ delta-target places B-grid call strikes 7–10pt OTM — close enough that a brief AM rally of ≤15pt triggers call stops before the session reversal.** Two call stops on Entries #5 and #6 (short call 7,680) fired when SPX hit 7,684.37 (4.37pt above strike), then SPX reversed to 7,618.88 — a 65.5pt intraday range with the stops triggered in the first 12.17pt of that range. This creates a perverse outcome: larger ES gaps that produce lower corrected cash opens (ostensibly more favorable for call viability) simultaneously compress OTM distance to dangerous levels on a 5pt Brandon spread. The favorable-for-call-credit and the dangerous-for-call-stop signals are structurally identical for ES gap ≥ 85pt correcting to SPX open 7,660–7,685.
