1. **The B-grid schedule anomaly (12:15–14:45 vs. canonical 09:45–12:45) has two distinct P&L consequence modes documented in W42:** (a) *Whitout mode* — on quiet narrow-range days (Oct 12 session range 24pt, Oct 13 range 27pt), the late schedule places entries after time decay has collapsed premium on 5pt Brandon spreads, causing GEX blocks and credit-gate failures that would not have occurred at 09:45–11:15; (b) *Post-event accidental mode* — on high-range CPI/event days (Oct 14 range 95pt), the late schedule places entries after the large directional move has already occurred, enabling full-grid placements below the intraday sell-off with reduced stop risk. The anomaly's P&L effect is session-type-dependent: it damages quiet sessions and paradoxically benefits post-event sessions. This finding is specific to the Oct 12–16 anomalous schedule.

A `max_entry_time` parameter of 14:30 would have prevented this specific loss.

3. **Oct 16 (VIX open 15.92, VIX close 20.00, SPX −93pt from open, intraday high 7,755.18 → low 7,631.94) produced viable put credits of $0.15–$1.05 across all 6 anomalous-schedule slots while call credits were universally $0.00–$0.05, but `require-both-sides` abandoned all put revenue.** Specifically, Entry #3 (13:15) showed call $0.03 / put $1.05 — the highest confirmed put-credit on a zero-placement day in the W42 dataset. - **B-grid schedule anomaly has two documented P&L consequence modes (W42, Oct 12–16):** On narrow-range quiet days (Oct 12 range 24pt, Oct 13 range 27pt), the anomalous 12:15–14:45 grid places entries after time decay collapses 5pt Brandon spread premium, producing whitouts; on high-range post-event days (Oct 14, 95pt CPI range), the same late schedule places entries after the large directional move has already occurred, enabling full-grid placements with reduced stop risk (+$1,289.60). The anomaly's P&L effect is session-type-dependent: it systematically damages quiet sessions and paradoxically benefits post-event sessions.
