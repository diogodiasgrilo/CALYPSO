**Jul 2 mechanics:** Entry #2 was placed at 11:16 — ~3 hours before the 14:00–15:00 drop. With MKT-042 buffer decay (starts at 2.5× over 4 hours), by ~14:30 the effective put buffer on Entry #2 would have decayed from 2.5× to approximately 1.4× of the base $1.75 put buffer = ~$2.45 effective buffer. At $455 credit / 7c = $65/contract, the stop trigger = $65 + $2.45 × 100 = not directly comparable at the contract level without the exact formula values. The key point: **Entry #2 was closed (stop or TP) before expiry; Entry #1 at the same strike was not.** The difference is time: E#1 had more buffer decay working in its favor (placed earlier, more time elapsed = smaller effective buffer at the same clock time) — but wait, more time elapsed = buffer has decayed MORE toward 1×, meaning SMALLER stop buffer. If Entry #1 had a smaller effective buffer than Entry #2 at the moment of the afternoon drop, it should have stopped first. The fact that E#1 survived and E#2 was closed suggests E#2's total credit was higher ($455 vs $50 for E#1), making its stop price higher — and was hit first.

**Recommendation:** Execute the formal Z0 buffer review documented in `HYDRA_BUFFER_OPTIMIZATION.md`. Specifically: aggregate all Z0 days since Apr 27 (now 10+ weeks of data) and compute stop rate, stop P&L, and near-miss events (within 10pt of short strike). **Confidence:** MEDIUM (data shows near-miss on Jul 2; formal analysis requires the full dataset not in scope here)

When identical strikes are placed at both entry slots on a volatile day, E#2's higher credit creates a higher stop trigger that is hit first.
