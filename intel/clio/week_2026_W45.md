## Part 4: New Learnings for Strategy Memory

### W45 specific learnings:

1. **Nov 5 (VIX open 16.86 → intraday 28.09, SPX −328.88pt, −4.3% crash): The whipsaw filter (`whipsaw_range_skip_mult: 1.75`) correctly blocked all 6 PM-grid entries on the largest SPX decline in HYDRA's data record.** At VIX 16.86, expected move ≈ 143pt; threshold ≈ 250pt. By first PM-grid entry (12:15 ET), intraday range was already ~295pt — 46pt above threshold. This is the definitive data point confirming the whipsaw filter's behavior in a tail-event crash: it engages cleanly and early (before any entry fires) when the range exceeds threshold, generating $0.00 P&L on a day that would have produced catastrophic stop losses on any IC placement.

2. **The W45 FOMC sequence (Day 1 = Nov 4, Day 2 = Nov 5, T+1 = Nov 6) is absent from the shared strategy context FOMC calendar table, causing Apollo to issue incorrect FOMC classification on Nov 4 ("no FOMC events scheduled this week") and contributing to anomalous entry-attempt records on Nov 6 (a T+1 blackout day).** The missing November FOMC dates are a documentation gap in the shared strategy context, not a code defect — adding `Nov | Nov 4 | Nov 5 | Nov 6` to the calendar table is the complete fix. This is distinct from the Standing Correction's ghost-flag narrative (which describes a code-level misreading of a correctly-set config value).

3. **Nov 3 put stop mechanics confirm that the PM-schedule anomaly's direct loss vector on rally days is put-strike compression, not GEX blocking:** When SPX drifts +23pt from open to entry time (7,542 open → ~7,565 at 14:21 entry), the 8δ delta-target anchors the short put to the current (higher) SPX level, generating a strike at 7,555 — only 12.33pt below the open and 13pt above the session low (7,542). The canonical AM grid (09:45) would anchor put strikes to 7,542 SPX (at or near open), providing 30–50pt+ of cushion vs. the same session low. PM-schedule put stops on drift-up days are mechanistically identical to the W26 and W27 "E#2 put placed closer to session low than E#1 at same parameters" pattern, applied at the grid-wide level.

4. **IBKR execution failures reached 41% of all attempted slots in W45 (11/27), their highest documented rate across the epoch, clustering in mid-afternoon PM-grid time windows (12:26–14:55 ET) across Nov 3, Nov 4, and Nov 6.** The failure rate is 0% for the week's pre-11:00 ET period (no canonical-grid slots were attempted due to the PM-schedule anomaly, but Nov 6 attempted 13:45–14:45 and went 0/3). The cascade mechanism — where 3 of 6 slots failing on Nov 3 eliminated diversification and concentrated all risk in Entry #5's fatal put — demonstrates that execution reliability is a prerequisite for the grid-diversification principle underlying Variant B's multi-entry design. A grid with 50%+ execution failure rate is structurally equivalent to a 1–2 slot grid with concentrated single-entry risk.

5. **Nov 6's negative `Expired Credits` (−$890.00) and $0.00 `Total Credit` with $0.00 `Stop Loss Debits` and 0 entries placed represents the third confirmed instance of prior-period open-position debits being recorded in a subsequent day's `Expired Credits` column (after Nov 3: −$1,025.00 and Nov 4: −$1,310.00).** This is a systematic data pipeline behavior — not an isolated error — where legs opened on Nov 3 that settled adversely (put stop) generated debit flows that were sequentially recorded across Nov 4 and Nov 6. Future CLIO reports should treat any day with `Expired Credits < 0 AND Total Credit = $0 AND Stop Loss Debits = $0` as definitively a prior-period carryover debit row, not a fresh-entry loss, and exclude it from weekly performance statistics for the current week's fresh-activity analysis.
